When a folder or set of projects starts to feel cluttered or hard to review, prefer reorganising by **mode of work** over **subject area**:

- **Initiatives** — committed multi-step outcomes with real dependencies. One project per outcome. Project drops when the outcome ships.
- **Operations** — recurring admin and maintenance. Bills, renewals, scheduled data pulls, weekly meta-tasks.
- **Ideas / Reading / Someday-maybe** — uncommitted curiosities, reading queue, "consider X" items. Tagged by theme for filtering. Monthly review = scan, prune, promote to an Initiative when Mark commits.

**Why:** Subject-area buckets (e.g. "Business Evolution," "Website," "Pragmatic LLM Uses") tend to absorb both real commitments AND idea captures over time. The mix makes review mechanical — you can't tell at a glance whether you're looking at a commitment or a 14-month-old "huh, interesting" item. Mode-based splits restore that signal. Tags carry the subject dimension *inside* the Ideas bucket, so subject-area filtering is still one click away.

**How to apply:**
- Trigger: when Mark says a folder has "too many projects," that review "feels mechanical," or "I can't see the big picture," consider whether the underlying issue is subject-vs-mode confusion.
- Don't impose mode-based structure pre-emptively if subject buckets are working. Use only when symptoms appear.
- Recurring tasks always go to Operations regardless of subject — the cadence is what matters, not the topic.
- New committed multi-step work spawns a new Initiative project. The moment scope crystallises, promote from Ideas to a new Initiative.
