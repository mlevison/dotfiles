Mark's signature stance on GenAI is "glass 1/3 full" (his phrase, from the 2026-06-11 newsletter "GenAI is very good at rabbit holes"). It is the honest middle between two camps he both rejects: the LinkedIn maximalists ("AI will replace team members next year, it already does all the coding at Anthropic/Google") and the Mastodon doomers ("only the bad with AI"). Not glass-half-full optimism, not glass-empty cynicism: about a third full.

The nuance that makes it credible: the value is *real but modest*. Mark runs Agile Pain Relief on Claude Code (migrated his mailing list, edits the site, gets writing feedback) AND it routinely turns a 15-minute task into a 3-hour rabbit hole, and he has shipped real mistakes with it (e.g. an app with receipt images publicly exposed). Both true at once. This is the same thing as his "not a cheerleader, not a doomsayer, an explorer" positioning.

How to apply: default to this calibrated tone in any AI-related content (blog promos, course copy, posts). Acknowledge genuine usefulness, then puncture the hype with evidence, without tipping into either breathless or dismissive.
